Home design is more than decoration. Research shows it is a form of identity construction — a way people express who they are and who they want to become. Home Glamour starts from that insight.

Four concepts were tested before committing to one. The winning idea: a virtual home where users can design any room with real purchasable furniture, save items for later, and track price drops — so the dream stays alive even when the budget isn't there yet.

The result is a concept grounded in real user behaviour, tested at every stage, and designed around aspiration as much as affordability.

## What the research revealed

Our high level observations drawn from initial research:

- There is a disconnect between reality and the fantasy of renovation shows
- It is important to be self-sufficient
- "I value my home and it reflects me"
- Inspiration motivates people to be more creative

"Home design shows nourish my creative side."

Home design is deeply personal. Research by Gram-Hanssen and Bech-Danielsen (2004) connects interior design to identity construction — in the absence of fixed social structures, people create and express themselves through their living spaces. Affinity mapping reinforced this: viewers under 30 watch renovation shows to imagine an alternate existence; people seek design inspiration to spark their own creativity; others want to reclaim skills that allow them to be self-sufficient.

<!-- Visual: Affinity map excerpt -->

Decorating is a form of self-expression. That became the foundation for everything we designed.

## Concept testing

We developed seven concepts to address the needs and insights that emerged from research: expert reviews to address the need for self-sufficiency with professional guidance; virtual home, to express creativity even without a budget; hazards and accessibility to help create cozy and safe homes for every need; tutorials to promote crafts; eco-friendliness for sustainability; budgets to help achieve dreams; and home gnomes for a fun way of learning urban gardening.

All four concepts we took into testing received positive reviews. Ultimately, the virtual house concept was the one participants unanimously chose.

## Designing for Alex

How might we help our proto persona have a more interactive experience with home design and attain the things he wants?

Alex cannot purchase his own home at present, but he still likes to think about ideas for renovations he may engage in once he does. Decorating a virtual house — using an app that lets users furnish living spaces with items that can be purchased in real life — allows Alex to express his creativity and get feedback from friends. The dream stays alive even when the timing isn't right yet.

## Building Home Glamour

Sketching out how to help Alex fulfill his budgetless dreams: the app accommodates self-expression, collecting ideas, and social features like sharing, giving and getting feedback. It was also important to build in the ability to save or buy furniture and accessories — leaving the door open for the dream to eventually become real.

<!-- Visual: Home Glamour sketches -->

Alex's landing page is My Home, each room represented by its own artboard. While the landing page focuses on the user, it offers additional options in the bottom navigation: shopping, social feed, articles and videos, and notifications.

<!-- Visual: Home Glamour concept app -->

Adding a sofa is only one feature of this user flow. Alex has the option to either buy immediately or place a price drop tracker on the item — receiving a notification when the price drops and the sofa becomes more affordable.

## Solutions

Following first-click user testing, we rearranged the icons in the top section of the app. The settings icon replaced the profile icon — we assumed users would want to access their profile to change settings, but testing proved otherwise. We also realised users needed the share icon to publish and share room creations with followers, so the search icon was removed as it appeared secondary.

<!-- Visual: Iterations after usability testing -->

The top left of the app went through two iterations: we replaced the logo placeholder with an actual logo, then received feedback that users did not know how to go back. A back button was added and the logo removed.

## Next steps

Among future possibilities: retail partnerships, pay-to-feature items, commission on items purchased through the app, paid editorial articles, and paid recommendations. For HGTV specifically — no forced ads, more satisfied users, and the ability to promote products and services while engaging users through price notifications.

Further usability testing and a new collaboration feature — inviting creative users to work together on ideas — would be the next design priorities.
